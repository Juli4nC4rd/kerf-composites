Applications

Four situations where the map settles it.

Each case is written from the point of view of the person holding the part and facing a decision. It ends with the question the measurement answers.

Case A · Process

Validating a cure cycle or a process

You changed a temperature ramp, a dwell time, a tool or a stacking sequence. The part comes out to drawing, but you do not know what the change left through the thickness. Two parts, one per cycle, cut on the same section, give two maps comparable point by point. The difference between them is the effect of the process, separated from everything else.

Does the new cycle reduce stress in the plies that matter, or move it somewhere else?

Case B · Repair

Repair and remanufacturing

A bonded repair or a rework machining adds a thermal and mechanical history to a part that already had one. What matters is the state in the transition zone: between the original material and the added one, between the reworked surface and the core. A cut through the repair shows the stress profile on both sides of the interface, and the gap with the sound part.

Is the repaired zone in the same stress state as the rest of the part, or will a gradient load the interface in service?

Case C · Thickness

Thick parts and variable sections

In a thick part, the thermal gradient during cure is no longer negligible: core and skin do not cure at the same time. Surface measurements see nothing of what happens in the middle. The contour method gives the whole cross-section, including where the thickness changes, thanks to sacrificial fixtures that make the section constant for the cut.

Where is the stress peak through the thickness, and how far from the change of section?

Case D · Simulation

Data for digital twins

A cure model predicts residual stress. Without a measurement on the real cross-section, it is only validated by the overall distortion, which is an average. On a cross-ply laminate, the thermo-elastic simulation gives a peak of −72 MPa where the contour measurement finds −100 to −150 MPa (Karebasannanavar Ramachandrappa et al., Composites Part B: Engineering 299 (2025) 112422). The measured map sets the target, ply by ply, and locates the gap to correct in the model.

Does the model reproduce the measured distribution through the thickness, and if not, in which plies is the gap concentrated?

Practical

What is needed to start a measurement.

  • A part or a coupon that can be sacrificed: the method is destructive.
  • The stack, the material datasheet and, if possible, the cure cycle.
  • The question asked: it is what sets the cut plane.
  • If relevant, the model to calibrate and the expected data format.
