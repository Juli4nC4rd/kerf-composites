Method

The contour method, ply by ply.

A part cut in two relaxes. The shape taken by the cut surface is the imprint of the stresses that were there. The contour method reads that imprint and turns it back into a stress map over the whole cross-section.

Principle

Four steps.

  1. Wire cut

    The part is cut in two by wire electrical discharge machining, in one straight pass, without cutting force. Clamping is symmetric so that both halves relax freely and in the same way. The width of the slot left by the wire is called the kerf: it is the company's name.

  2. Measurement of both surfaces

    Both cut surfaces are measured point by point, with a touch probe or optical profilometry. Averaging the two readings cancels antisymmetric effects (wire path deviation, shear) and keeps only the relaxation due to stress.

  3. Calculation

    The averaged surface is smoothed, then applied as a reversed displacement to a finite element model of the half part. The calculation returns the stress normal to the cut plane as it existed before the cut. The amount of smoothing is set by an error criterion, not by eye.

  4. Map

    The result is a map of the normal stress over the whole cross-section, with its uncertainty. It reads like a drawing: one value at every point, on a scientific scale, with the cut artefact zones marked out.

Composites

What changes for a laminate.

Cutting

Wire EDM with sacrificial strips

Carbon fibres conduct well enough for wire electrical discharge machining, provided the arc starts and holds steadily. Sacrificial strips bonded at the entry and exit of the cut stabilise the arc and protect the edges. A cut without cracking or delamination has been demonstrated on CFRP [1].

Model

Ply-by-ply treatment

A laminate is not homogeneous: each ply has its own stiffness and orientation. The calculation model reproduces the real stack, ply by ply, with the orthotropic properties of each. The final map tells one ply from the next instead of averaging through the thickness.

Geometry

Real ply thicknesses

Nominal ply thicknesses are not those of the cured part. Calculating with thicknesses measured on the cross-section, rather than those of the material datasheet, changes stress peaks by 24 to 36 % [1]. So we record the real stack before calculating.

Fixture

Sacrificial fixtures for variable sections

A section that changes along the cut (stiffener, radius, thickness step) unbalances the relaxation and creates artefacts. A sacrificial fixture, machined and bonded around the part, makes the section seen by the wire constant. It is then removed from the calculation.

Deliverable

What a report contains.

Annotated map
The normal stress over the cross-section, on a scientific scale, with the stack overlaid and the zones of interest marked.
Correction method
The corrections applied (reference cut, artefact masking, smoothing retained) are described with their parameters, so that the result can be reproduced.
Uncertainty
An uncertainty at every point, derived from measurement noise, the smoothing choice and material properties. It is plotted with the map, not left in an appendix.
Artefact bands
Cut edges and zones where relaxation is disturbed are marked out. Values there are given as indicative and flagged as such.
Validation

What the literature says.

Twenty years

The contour method has been established for twenty years and cross-checked against independent neutron measurements in interlaboratory comparisons.

0.35 to 0.5 mm

Spatial resolution obtained through the thickness of a laminate [3].

−72 MPa

Peak predicted by a thermo-elastic model on a cross-ply laminate, where the contour measurement gives −100 to −150 MPa [2].
